Hanging Flags Vertically

Keep the canton, the upper corner next to the pole, at the top left as you look at it. The hoist edge goes to the top, and what was the top stripe ends up on your left.

For most flags that means a quarter turn and then turning the flag over, so you see its back. The United States, Canada and most flag references follow this convention. The Union Flag and flags with writing or emblems are exceptions.

Exceptions

Flags that don't follow the turn-over rule

United Kingdom

The Union Flag only rotates

The Union Flag is not symmetrical. In the corner next to the pole, the broad white diagonal must sit above the red one, and the Flag Institute says this still applies when it hangs vertically: the broad white diagonal goes uppermost in the top-left corner. Turning the flag over would put it upside down, so give it the quarter turn and stop there.

Writing on the flag

Text has to stay readable

Saudi Arabia's flag carries the shahada and is made double-sided so the text reads correctly, right to left, from either side. Turning it sideways leaves the text running vertically. Purpose-made vertical versions with the inscription and sword rotated are reported, and some sources say the flag should not be hung vertically at all. Iraq, Iran, Brazil and others also carry writing. For any of these, use a purpose-made vertical flag or check the country's own protocol.

Emblems and crowns

Some emblems stay upright

Flags of the World describes Liechtenstein's vertical flag as the horizontal design turned a quarter turn anticlockwise, with the crown kept upright in the top-left corner. Vertical banners are common in Central Europe, and versions that carry a coat of arms are generally made with the arms upright. If a flag has an emblem or seal, buy the vertical version instead of turning a horizontal one.

Convention, not world law

The rule assumes a canton

Top left for the canton is a widely followed convention, written into US and Canadian guidance. It is not an international law, and it says little about flags with no honour point in the corner. For official or ceremonial displays, the country's own flag rules decide.

Where it hangs

Walls, windows and streets

Flat against a wall

Judge it from where people stand. The canton goes at their top left. Under the US Flag Code the union goes uppermost and to the flag's own right, which is the observer's left, whether the flag hangs horizontally or vertically.

In a window

The audience is outside. Hang it so the canton is at the top left for someone in the street, which means it looks reversed from inside the room. The US Flag Code treats a window the same way as a wall, from the street side.

Over the middle of a street

People see it from both sides, so the US Flag Code fixes it by compass: hang it vertically with the union to the north on an east–west street, and to the east on a north–south street.

On a pole

Flown horizontally from a pole, the hoist is always against the pole and the flag reads normally from the front. Check which stripe or colour belongs on top: several flags look like another country's when upside down.

Dimensions

Official proportions and shop sizes

Each country sets its own ratio of height (hoist) to length (fly). Most shops sell every national flag at one size, usually 3 × 5 ft (90 × 150 cm), whatever the official ratio, so a 1:2 flag like Canada's or a 2:3 flag like France's is slightly stretched or squashed. If the proportions matter, look for a flag sold at the country's official ratio. Each entry in the list gives sizes at the official ratio.

Where to buy

Buying a flag

Each country entry links to a search for that flag on Amazon (US and UK), eBay, Etsy and Walmart. These are searches, not endorsements. Before you buy, check the listing for:

  • Material: printed polyester for indoor or occasional use, nylon or sewn polyester for flying outdoors.
  • The size and ratio you want, and whether it matches the official proportions.
  • A vertical-format version if the flag has an emblem or writing and you will hang it vertically.
  • Fittings: a canvas heading with brass grommets for a pole, or a sleeve for a crossbar or indoor pole.

Specialist dealers stock every nation. In the US, CRW Flags carries flags of all nations in several sizes and is an authorized Annin retailer. Annin's international flags are also sold through general retailers.

Before you step back

Quick check

  • The hoist edge, the one with the sleeve, grommets or heading tape, is at the top.
  • The canton, or the stripe that used to be on top, is on the viewer's left.
  • For the Union Flag: the broad white diagonal is above the red one in the top-left corner.
  • Any writing reads normally, and any crown, seal or coat of arms is the right way up. If not, you need a vertical-format flag.
  • You checked it from where the audience will stand, which is outside for a window.
